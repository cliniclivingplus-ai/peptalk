Mechanisms and Metabolic Pathways

Lipolysis

What is it:

  • Lipolysis is the breakdown of stored fat (triglycerides) from the fat cells into free fatty acids and glycerol, which your body is then able to use for energy.

Why does it matter:

  • This is essentially the process that allows your body to "burn fat." It releases the fatty acids from the cells where your body can then burn them off.

Where this is used:

  • AOD-9604

  • Growth Hormone Secretagogues

Lipogenesis

What is it:

  • Lipogenesis is the creation of new fat and can be due to excess carbohydrates, proteins, or alcohol.

Why does it matter:

  • It's basically the opposite of lipolysis. Things that can reduce insulin levels or improve insulin sensitivity can inhibit this process and potentially help prevent weight gain or things like fatty liver.

Where this is used:

  • 5-Amino-1MQ

  • MOTS-C

  • GLPs

AMPK Pathway (AMP-activated protein kinase)

What is it:

  • AMPK is kind of like fuel gauge in your body. If your energy is low, it turns on fat burning and turns off fat storage.

Why does it matter:

  • It can help with weight loss, blood sugar control, and longevity. It's really one of the major pathways that gets targeted through some of the metabolic peptides.

Where this is used:

  • MOTS-C

  • 5-Amino-1MQ

mTOR Pathway (Mechanistic Target of Rapamycin)

What is it:

  • mTOR is a growth and repair signal in the body that can be triggered by things like protein, insulin, and IGF-1. It tells your body to build muscle and regenerate tissue.

Why does it matter:

  • Ideally, we're activating mTOR after workouts to help grow muscle. On the flipside, constantly elevating this can lead to faster aging and inflammation.

Where this is used:

  • IGF-1 LR3

  • Growth Hormone Secretagogues

Autophagy

What is it:

  • Autophagy can be described as the body's clean-up system and will break down and recycle damaged cells, protein, and parts of the mitochondria.

Why does it matter:

  • This process helps keep the healthy cells in the body, can help prevent diseases, and can be a pretty big piece in slowing aging.

Where this is used:

  • Epitalon

  • FOXO4-DRI

Insulin Sensitivity / Resistance

What is it:

  • Insulin sensitivity is how well your body responds to insulin, which is the hormone that helps to get the glucose out of your blood and into your cells.

  • Insulin resistance is when your cells aren't responding well to insulin and your body then has to make more of it to accomplish the same goal.

Why does it matter:

  • When you're insulin resistance, your fat loss is going to slow down, your blood sugar is going to stay high, and your energy levels are going to be in the dumpster. Peptides that improve insulin sensitivity can help lower blood sugar, reduce fat storage, and make it easier to achieve better body composition (less fat, more muscle).

Where this is used:

  • GLPs

Glucagon Signaling

What is it:

  • Glucagon is a hormone that raises blood sugar when levels are too low and causes fat to breakdown in the body.

Why does it matter:

  • Peptides that target the glucagon receptor (Retatrutide, Mazdutide, Survodutide) help to burn fat by boosting energy expenditure and increasing lipolysis.

Where this is used:

  • Retatrutide

  • Survodutide, Mazdutide

Leptin Signaling

What is it:

  • Leptin is a hormone that is made by fat cells that tell your brain that you have enough energy stored (which acts like a satiety signal for the body).

Why does it matter:

  • Leptin signaling can tell your brain when it's time to stop eating. A lot of times, people are leptin resistance so their body is ignoring the signal causing them to overeat.

Where this is used:

  • GLPs

Adiponectin

What is it:

  • Adiponectin is a hormone made by fat cells that will improve insulin sensitivity and increase fat oxidation.

Why does it matter:

  • Higher levels of this hormone can help with easier fat loss, better blood sugar control, and reduced inflammation in the body.

Where this is used:

  • MOTS-C

  • GLPs

Ghrelin Pathway

What is it:

  • Ghrelin is your body's hunger hormone that tells you when to eat. It can also stimulate growth hormone release in the body.

Why does it matter:

  • Ghrelin agonists like the GHRPs or MK-677 will leverage this pathway as a means to boost growth hormone production in the body by directly targeting this. It will also increase hunger as a result.

Where this is used:

  • GHRP-2, GHRP-6

  • MK-677

GLP-1 (Glucagon-like Peptide 1)

What is it:

  • GLP-1 is a gut hormone that helps your body regulate blood sugar, increase satiety (feeling of fullness), and slow digestion.

Why does it matter:

  • It helps to lower blood sugar, reduce appetite, and support weight loss. This is where it comes into play in things like Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, and Retatrutide.

Where this is used:

  • GLPs

GIP (Glucose-dependent Insulinotropic Polypeptide)

What is it:

  • GIP helps your body release insulin as a response to eating food, especially when that food is carbohydrates (mmmm donuts).

Why does it matter:

  • When you combine this with a GLP-1, it can improve blood sugar control and support fat loss more effectively than GLP-1 by itself.

Where this is used:

  • GLPs

FGF21 (Fibroblast Growth Factor 21)

What is it:

  • FGF21 is a hormone that helps regulate metabolism, especially alongside of fasting or low-carb diets and can promote fat burning and improve insulin sensitivity.

Why does it matter:

Where this is used:

PGC-1a (Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha)

What is it:

  • This regulates mitochondrial function and fat metabolism and is turned on by the body during exercise, cold exposure, and fasting.

Why does it matter:

  • More of this means that your body is using energy better, oxidizing fat better, and gives better endurance.

Where this is used:

  • MOTS-C

SIRT1 Pathway

What is it:

  • SIRT1 is a gene that can help with stress resistance, fat metabolism, and aging and gets activated during fasting and calorie restriction.

Why does it matter:

  • It's an important piece when we consider longevity and can promote autophagy, metabolic flexibility, and inflammation control.

Where this is used:

  • MOTS-C

  • Epitalon

Akt/PKB Pathway

What is it:

  • Akt (also known as Protein Kinase B), is a signal that promotes growth and glucose uptake in cells.

Why does it matter:

  • This can help cells absorb nutrients and grow and has an important role in muscle growth, anti-aging, and insulin sensitivity.

Where this is used:

  • IGF-1 LR3

  • Growth Hormone Secretagogues

ERK/MAPK Pathway

What is it:

  • This is a pathway that can control cell division, growth, and repair and is a piece of how cells respond to external signals like growth factors.

Why does it matter:

  • Some peptides can trigger this pathway to support tissue regeneration, cognitive performance, and anti-aging.

Where this is used:

  • P-21

POMC Neurons (Pro-opiomelanocortin)

What is it:

  • POMC neurons are appetite-suppressing neurons in the hypothalamus and can reduce hunger and increase energy use when active.

Why does it matter:

  • GLP-s and other satiety peptides can activate POMC neurons to help reduce food intake and put you into a caloric deficit without feeling insanely hungry.

Where this is used:

  • GLPs

  • Cagrilintide

NPY/AgRP Neurons

What is it:

  • These do the opposite of POMC and will increase hunger and reduce energy usage and can be activated during fasting or calorie restriction.

Why does it matter:

  • Peptides that suppress these hunger neurons can help to reduce cravings and hunger.

Where this is used:

  • GLPs

  • Cagrilintide

  • Th1 and Th2 are two pieces of your immune system. Th1 will help with cellular defense and take care of things like viruses and bacteria. Th2 will help with the humoral response and take care of things like allergies and parasites.

Why does it matter:

  • If Th2 is overactive and Th1 is underactive, then you can have issues like allergies, autoimmune issues, and weak defenses against infections.

Regulatory T Cells (Tregs)

What is it:

  • Tregs keep your body from attacking your own tissues and help to shut off inflammation after an immune response is over. Think of these like peacekeepers within the immune system to prevent unnecessary fighting.

Why does it matter:

  • Low or lack of functional Tregs can lead to autoimmune disease and chronic inflammation.

Where this is used:

  • Thymalin

Toll-Like Receptors (TLRs)

What is it:

  • TLRs are sensors that exist on immune cells to identify danger with things like viruses or bacterial toxins. They essentially alert the immune system to those issues.

Why does it matter:
