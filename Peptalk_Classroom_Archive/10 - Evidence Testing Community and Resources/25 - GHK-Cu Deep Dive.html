Overview

GHK-Cu is a peptide that is already naturally produced by the body and typically is utilized for benefits like skin, wound healing, hair, and anti-aging. It is a regulator of thousands of genes within the body and has a lot of potential benefits. As we age, our levels start to decline significantly, which puts GHK-Cu as potentially one of the most beneficial anti-aging peptides out there.

Mechanism of Action

There are really a couple of distinct mechanisms that are benefits of GHK-Cu.

  1. Copper Delivery - GHK has an extremely high affinity for copper peptides meaning they WANT to be together. GHK is going to grab the copper and shuttle it into cells that are deficient. This is what drives a lot of downstream consequences because copper is an essential cofactor for dozens of enzyme systems (basically things that fuel other processes) throughout the body. If copper is low within specific tissues, then those downstream processes can start to degrade. This occurs as we age. Some of those processes include:

    • Collagen cross-linking - this is where collagen fibers are woven together and connected to things within skin, tendons, organs, etc. There is an enzyme that helps with this, lysyl oxidase, which NEEDS coper to function. If it doesn't then the collagen that is produced isn't woven together as well. This means you will have weaker, less elastic tissue which leads to things like aging skin and slow healing wounds. When GHK-Cu helps improve this copper supply, the collagen structure is stronger. Think about it like a tightly woven net and how strong that can be vs. one more open.

    • Antioxidant Defense - There's an antioxidant enzyme called superoxide dismutase which helps get rid of damaging free radicals produced by the mitochondria. This again requires copper to work well, so if copper drops, then oxidative stress increases, and your cells will ultimately age faster. If you remember from the mitochondria course, we talked about needing to clean up the ROS within the cells, but this basically helps to mitigate the amount of cleanup you'll have to do.

    • Wound Healing - There are multiple enzymes that are going to be at the site of a tissue injury that need copper to function such as the ones that help with new extracellular matrix, regulating inflammatory signaling, and replacing scar tissue with healthy tissue. GHK-Cu is supporting the processes that will ultimately make an injury heal faster, cleaner, and better.

The important thing to understand here with all of these is that as we age, our GHK-Cu levels will decline, and so will our ability to deliver copper to all of those processes above (and more). Our collagen quality will degrease, our antioxidant abilities will decrease, our wounds will heal slower, etc. They don't just stop one day. Over time, they will just continue to decrease, which is exactly how we age. You don't wake up one day with wrinkles.

  1. Gene Modulation - GHK-Cu can modulate the expression of over 4,000 human gees within the body. It basically can help to do the opposite of what we see happening with genes as we age. So it will basically boost the genes that help with tissue repair, antioxidant defense, DNA repair, and mitochondrial function while turning down the ones that may cause inflammation, tumor progression, and tissue breakdown.

    • NF-kB Suppression - NF-kB is the driver of inflammatory signaling within your cells (see the Immune System course) and can be driven by things like injury, stress, infections, etc. to turn on inflammatory responses. This is great as a short term response. However, chronic activation is one of the biggest drivers of lower levels of persistent inflammation that is involved in things like cardiovascular disease, metabolic dysfunction, neurodegeneration, and more. GHK-Cu can suppress NF-kB activity and helping to reduce overall inflammation throughout the body.

    • SIRT1 Activation - If you remember from the mitochondria course, SIRT1 is a protein that helps with cell maintenance in things like DNA repair, mitochondrial biogenesis, and metabolic efficiency. GHK-Cu activates it differently than how something like NAD+ would, meaning that you could combine the two of these together and they would work synergistically together for those benefits.

Potential MMP Misunderstandings

One thing that you may encounter is a discussion about increasing the production of MMPs which breaks down existing collagen. So you may become confused in how GHK-Cu is stimulating new collagen production, but also breaking down existing collagen. The important thing to understand here is that MMPs are going to help get rid of the damaged collagen that you don't want within your body. So instead of just making new and throwing it in with the bad, GHK-Cu is cleaning up the old and replacing it with the new.

Benefits of GHK-Cu

GHK-Cu is going to have a broad set of benefits that you may have been able to pick up on prior to this point. I'm going to break down the major ones that this is typically used for.

Skin Quality and Anti-Aging

This is probably the most commonly desired benefit of GHK-Cu. GHK-Cu can help with things like wrinkle depth, skin firmness, elasticity, and texture. In one trial, they observed GHK-Cu with a 31.6% reduction in wrinkle volume over 8 weeks compared to a Matrixyl control which is used commonly as an anti-aging comparison compound. The mechanism that drives this is what was mentioned at the end of the last section with new collagen production alongside of the removal of damaged collagen.

GHK-Cu can also help with repairing the skin's barrier proteins, reducing photodamage and hyperpigmentation, and improving skin clarity. But it's not just something that happens topically. These processes are occurring at the structural level within the extracellular matrix.

One important thing to understand with this benefit is that it takes months for collagen remodeling to occur. You didn't get your wrinkles in a week, so don't expect them to go away that fast.

Wound Healing / Tissue Repair

GHK-Cu can accelerate wound closure, help with the integration of skin in transplants, reduce the formation of scars, and promote angiogenesis (formation of new blood vessels) which is critical to supply nutrients and oxygen where they need to go. You have a few different ways in which all of this occurs which is the delivery of copper to the wound-site enzymes, collagen production signaling, and MMP-mediated clearance of the scar-forming fibrous tissue. GHK-Cu is basically targeting all of the pieces of the healing process at once resulting in faster closure, less scarring, and better quality tissue at the wound site.


Hair Growth

GHK-Cu is quite effective in hair growth and can help with follicle size as well as the growth phase of the hair cycle (see Hair Loss course for more information). It can help to enlarge hair follicles and extend the anagen (active growth) phase and increase the thickness of the hair. They actually have done a study with GHK-Cu applied topically and daily for 6 months with significant hair growth improvement.

The mechanism for this is from the copper-dependent enzymes involved with follicle function as well as GHK-Cu's effects on growth factor signaling which can help support blood supply to the follicles. Ultimately it will help with slowing loss and even creating better, denser, hair quality.

Systemic Anti-Aging

The gene modulation component of GHK-Cu suggest that GHK-Cu can reverse some of the gene expression that basically make up aging at the cellular level. So instead of just masking the symptoms, it's actually changing the biological processes that drive it.

The NF-kB suppression and SIRT1 activation are going to be systemic changes from GHK-Cu. So instead of just having anti-inflammatory benefits in the skin for example, they're happening throughout the body. This is where injectable administration of GHK-Cu can shine over topical application if you're looking for benefits outside of targeted areas.


Dosage Strategies and Protocols

GHK-Cu is primarily administered in two main forms: topical (creams, serums) and injectable. Topical works well for localized benefits for skin and hair, where injectable can be used for systemic benefits throughout the body.

Topical:

  • 1% for facial use,

  • 2% for body application,

  • 3% for promoting hair growth.

  • One important thing here is you don't HAVE to make it any more concentrated for these other applications. 300mg in 1oz of Hyaluronic Acid is generally sufficient for all applications.

Injectable, oral, intranasal (injectable is going to be the most effective here):

1 - 2 mg daily for 4 - 12 weeks on, followed by 4 - 8 weeks off. My preferred protocol is 2 mg daily for 4 - 8 weeks.

Side Effects and Safety

GHK-Cu seems to have a pretty good safety profile overall, especially for the topical applications.

Potential Side Effects Include:

  • Injection site reactions - GHK-Cu can be quite painful at the injection site. There are a few main ways to mitigate this.

    • Go deeper. Use a 1/2" needle instead of 5/16"

    • Dilute with more BAC water

    • Inject in the glutes instead of stomach

    • Use a massage gun on the site after injecting

  • Copper uglies - This is VERY rare, but basically people are reporting that their skin looks worse before it gets better. This may be from using too much for the individual and dialing it back some can potentially help so using a smaller dosage or less concentrated serum can help. But in some cases, it could be that the MMP component is causing a breakdown of collagen faster than it can be replaced. Another potential cause of this is "purge" before the improvement, similar to how Accutane works.

  • Copper accumulation - This is quite unlikely to occur based on the amount of copper that is actually within GHK-Cu, but if someone used high amounts for long periods of time, potentially this could be a concern. But like I said, it is very unlikely.
