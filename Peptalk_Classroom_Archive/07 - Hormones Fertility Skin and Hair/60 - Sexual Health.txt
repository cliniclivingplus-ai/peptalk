Sexual health is something that many people struggle with and it is also one of the most misunderstood. The usual approach to sexual dysfunction, the PDE5 inhibitors like Viagra and Cialis, works on the plumbing. They improve blood flow to the genitals so an erection is physically possible (works for women too!). But blood flow is only a small piece of it, and for many, not even the true underlying issue. For a lot of people, the limiting factor is not the plumbing at all. It is desire itself, the wanting, the spark, the mental and emotional readiness that has to come before physical arousal even matters.

This is the gap the libido peptides address, and it is why they have generated so much interest. Compounds like PT-141 and kisspeptin work upstream, in the brain, on the actual circuits that generate desire and arousal in the first place. That is a different and complementary approach to the blood-flow drugs. Oxytocin works on connection, intimacy, and the quality of the experience. Understanding which compound targets which part of the sexual response is the key to using any of them well, because they solve different problems, and using the wrong one for your situation is a common and avoidable mistake.

This course covers the major compounds in this space: PT-141 (the strongest-evidence libido peptide), oxytocin (the most asked about and most misunderstood), the PDE5 inhibitors like tadalafil, kisspeptin (for hormonal regulation, and the troche delivery format that combines several of these. It also covers something that deserves its own attention, which is how libido changes through menopause and the hormonal transitions, because that is a huge part of the real-world picture and it connects directly to the HRT material in the Hormone Course.

The Components of Sexual Response

Human sexual response is not a single switch. It is a sequence of distinct processes, each of which can work well or poorly somewhat independently of the others.

Desire (libido): the psychological wanting, meaning interest in sex, sexual thoughts and fantasies, the motivation to seek intimacy. This starts in the brain, driven by neurotransmitters (especially dopamine) and hormones (testosterone and estrogen in both sexes). When desire is the problem, no amount of blood-flow help does anything, because the wanting is not there to begin with. No matter how good YOU might think something is, if I don't want it, then it won't happen. Without fixing the desire component of this, nothing else can really occur. 

Arousal (the physical response): the body's physical readiness, meaning erection in men, lubrication and genital engorgement in women. This is largely a vascular, blood-flow process, driven by nitric oxide and the relaxation of blood vessels. This is what the PDE5 inhibitors target. 

Orgasm and resolution: the peak of the experience and what follows. Oxytocin surges at orgasm and is involved in its intensity and in the bonding and contentment afterward.

Connection and intimacy: the emotional and relational side that wraps around all of it, meaning trust, closeness, bonding. This is heavily oxytocin-driven and is a real part of sexual satisfaction. 

The Brain vs the Body

Here is the distinction that reframes this whole area. Traditional erectile dysfunction drugs work peripherally, on the body, specifically the blood vessels of the genitals. They do nothing for desire. They simply make the physical response possible if desire and arousal are already there. This is why so many people are let down by them. A man with low libido who takes Viagra may get the physical capacity for an erection but still have no interest in using it, because the drug never touched the actual problem. This is also why Tadalafil can be used as a FANTASTIC pre-workout type of compound, because without the desire and arousal, this compound isn't really doing much "down there."

The libido peptides work centrally, on the brain, on the circuits that generate desire and arousal in the first place. That is a different mechanism, and it is why they can help people the blood-flow drugs cannot, people whose limiting factor is the wanting, not the plumbing. Working out whether your limiting factor is central (desire) or peripheral (blood flow), or both, is the most useful piece of self-assessment in this whole area, and it decides which compound actually makes sense for you.



PT-141 (Bremelanotide)

Text saved

What it targets: Desire and arousal (central, brain).

Mechanism: Melanocortin (MC4) receptor agonist in the hypothalamus.

Evidence level: Strongest in this class. FDA-approved (Vyleesi) for women.

Best for: Low libido and HSDD in women; low desire and PDE5 partial-response in men.

Typical dosing: 1.75mg subcutaneous (approved dose) about 45 minutes before activity, as needed. HOWEVER, if you look at the Consolidated Dosage, Reconstitution, and Cycle Lengths Course, you will see that my opinion is that you should start SIGNIFICANTLY lower to assess tolerance and effects. 

Route: Subcutaneous injection or nasal spray

Side effects: Nausea, flushing, headache; a transient rise in blood pressure. These are the reasons why I think starting lower is best. Many take this and get these side effects and never want to try again. 

PT-141, also called bremelanotide, is the main libido peptide and the one with by far the strongest evidence behind it. Its story connects directly to the Melanotan course. PT-141 came out of Melanotan II, the tanning peptide, when researchers noticed that male volunteers in the early tanning studies had an unexpected side effect, spontaneous sexual arousal. That observation redirected a whole drug development program toward sexual function, and the sexual effect was isolated and refined into bremelanotide.

How It Works

PT-141 is a melanocortin receptor agonist. It activates melanocortin receptors, most importantly the MC4 receptor, in the hypothalamus and central nervous system. These are the brain circuits that regulate sexual motivation and arousal. Importantly, this is not a blood-flow mechanism. PT-141 acts upstream in the brain, generating desire and arousal at their source rather than helping the physical plumbing. Downstream, melanocortin activation boosts dopamine signaling in the brain's reward pathway and increases oxytocin release, both of which feed into sexual desire and arousal.

Someone whose problem is genuinely low desire, where the wanting itself is missing, is not helped by a blood-flow drug, but may be helped by PT-141 because it works on desire directly. It is also why PT-141 can produce desire and arousal somewhat independently of physical stimulation, which is useful precisely when lack of libido is the barrier.

The Evidence

PT-141 is FDA-approved as Vyleesi, granted in 2019 for hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women, one of only two FDA-approved treatments for low sexual desire in women. The approval rested on Phase 3 trials showing statistically significant improvements in sexual desire and reductions in the distress that comes with low desire, measured on validated scales. Effects are usually felt within 30 to 60 minutes of injection and last several hours. It is used when wanted, not taken daily. 

For men, PT-141 is used off-label. An older randomized controlled trial found bremelanotide could rescue erectile response in men who had not responded well to sildenafil (Viagra). More recently, a 2024 observational study by Goldstein and Goldstein of 21 men with various sexual dysfunctions using as-needed bremelanotide found high satisfaction. Around 80 percent reported greater satisfaction with their experience, over 70 percent found sex more pleasurable, and 86 percent felt more at ease initiating intimacy. There is particular clinical interest for men on TRT whose libido stays low despite normalized testosterone, and for men who are partial responders to PDE5 inhibitors.

Dosing and Use

Approved and standard protocol (from the Vyleesi label and clinical use):

1.75mg subcutaneous injection, roughly 45 minutes before anticipated activity, used as needed.

Do not exceed one dose per 24 hours, and no more than eight doses per month.

Compounded intranasal formulations are also used (usually dosed 30 to 60 minutes before activity).

As-needed dosing before activity is the important piece here. This is not a daily medication.

Side Effects and Cautions

PT-141's side effects come from the fact that it activates melanocortin receptors broadly, not just the ones involved in sex.

Nausea: the most common side effect, sometimes significant, driven by the melanocortin mechanism (the same reason Melanotan II causes nausea). Starting at a lower dose and finding your tolerance helps. Some people get ahead of it with anti-nausea strategies like Ondasteron (Zofran) that you can get easily from AlgoRX.

Flushing and headache: common and generally short-lived, related to the central and vascular effects.

Transient blood pressure increase: PT-141 causes a temporary rise in blood pressure and a small drop in heart rate after dosing.

Skin darkening: with repeated use, because of the shared melanocortin (MC1R) tanning mechanism. This is more of a Melanotan-family effect but possible, especially at higher frequencies of use.

Safety

PT-141 is not appropriate for people with uncontrolled high blood pressure or known cardiovascular disease, because of the transient blood pressure increase it causes.



Oxytocin

Text saved

What it targets: Intimacy, connection, orgasm quality (not primarily desire).

Mechanism: Oxytocin receptor activation; bonding and orgasm pathways.

Evidence level: Mixed. Stronger for orgasm and intimacy than for desire.

Best for: Emotional closeness, orgasm intensity, intimacy quality, as an adjunct.

Typical dosing: 10 to 40 IU intranasal, 30 to 60 minutes before activity, as needed.

Route: Intranasal spray (for the CNS effect); also troches and sublingual.

Main side effects: Generally mild; well tolerated short-term.

Oxytocin is popularly known as the love hormone or bonding hormone, released during hugging, touch, childbirth, breastfeeding, and orgasm. That reputation leads a lot of people to assume it is a straightforward libido booster. The reality is more nuanced and more interesting, and getting this right is the main reason to read this section carefully.

What Oxytocin Actually Is

Oxytocin is a natural hormone and neuropeptide made in the hypothalamus and released by the pituitary. It plays a central role in social bonding, trust, empathy, and attachment, and it is involved in sexual function. It is released during arousal, peaks at orgasm, and supports the emotional bonding that goes with intimate contact. In the body, oxytocin also triggers nitric oxide production in a way that supports erection in men and engorgement in women. So it is a real part of the sexual response. The question is what supplementing it from outside actually does.

The Evidence

Animal studies consistently support a role for oxytocin in sexual behavior. But human clinical trials, particularly the ones looking at libido and sexual initiation with intranasal oxytocin, have produced genuinely mixed results. The largest randomized controlled trial in women showed no improvement in sexual function versus placebo. The evidence that oxytocin boosts desire or starts arousal is weak and inconsistent. 

Where the evidence is stronger is orgasm and intimacy quality, not desire. Controlled trials of intranasal oxytocin have shown greater orgasm intensity in both sexes and increased sexual satiety and contentment after intercourse in men. A well-known couples study found that oxytocin did not change classical parameters like sexual drive, arousal, or erection, but it did increase the intensity of orgasm, the contentment afterward, and aspects of partner interaction, with women feeling more relaxed and better able to share sexual desires. The effects were small to moderate and centered on the experience and connection, not on generating raw desire.

Oxytocin is best understood as an adjunct for emotional intimacy, connection, and the quality of the sexual experience, including orgasm intensity and post-orgasm closeness, rather than as a standalone libido booster. If the main problem is low desire, PT-141 has far stronger evidence, and oxytocin is the wrong tool. If the goal is deepening connection and improving the quality and intimacy of sex in a relationship, oxytocin has a more reasonable use-case. This distinction is the single most important thing to understand about oxytocin.

Why Intranasal Delivery

Oxytocin for these purposes is usually given intranasally, and there is a real mechanistic reason. As covered in the Semax course, intranasal delivery of peptides lets them reach the brain directly through the olfactory and trigeminal nerve pathways, bypassing the blood-brain barrier, which is essential for a CNS-targeted effect. Intravenous oxytocin largely stays in the periphery and does not reach the brain well, so for the central bonding and intimacy effects, the nasal route is the standard. This is also why oxytocin is a natural fit for troche and sublingual delivery.

Dosing and Safety

Typical as-needed protocol:

10 to 40 IU intranasal, roughly 30 to 60 minutes before anticipated activity (some sources use up to about 40 IU). For reference, 1IU is approximately 2mcg for Oxytocin. Therefore, 10-40 IU is going to be about 20-80 mcg. 

Subcutaneous dosing (100 to 300 mcg) is also described, but intranasal is preferred for the central intimacy effects.

As-needed before activity is preferred over daily scheduled use.

Oxytocin is generally well tolerated in the short term with mild side effects. Long-term daily use is less well characterized, and the receptor-desensitization concern is the main reason to keep it as needed rather than constant.



Tadalafil and the PDE5 Inhibitors

Text saved

What it targets: Arousal and erection (peripheral, blood flow).

Mechanism: PDE5 inhibition, which sustains nitric oxide and vasodilation.

Evidence level: Very strong. Decades of RCTs, FDA-approved.

Best for: Erectile dysfunction (the physical, vascular component).

Tadalafil dosing: As-needed (10 to 20mg) or low-dose daily (2.5 to 5mg).

Route: Oral.

Main side effects: Headache, flushing, nasal congestion, indigestion.

The PDE5 inhibitors are the well-known erectile dysfunction drugs: sildenafil (Viagra), tadalafil (Cialis), vardenafil (Levitra), and others. They are in this course not because they are peptides (they are not) but because they are fantastic additions to y our toolkit. Tadalafil gets specific attention because its long duration and daily-dosing option make it especially versatile for both sexual health and exercise performance. 

How They Work

An erection is fundamentally a blood-flow event. Sexual stimulation triggers the release of nitric oxide in the genitals, which relaxes the smooth muscle of the blood vessels, letting blood flow in and produce an erection. An enzyme called PDE5 breaks down the signaling molecule (cGMP) that keeps this relaxation going, ending the erection. PDE5 inhibitors block that enzyme, so the relaxation and blood flow are sustained. Importantly, they do not create an erection out of nothing. They need sexual stimulation and the nitric oxide it triggers to work. They amplify and sustain the physical response. They do not generate desire.

This is also the main limitation with them. PDE5 inhibitors are purely peripheral. They work on the plumbing. If the problem is a vascular erection issue, they are highly effective. If the problem is low desire, they do nothing for the actual issue. They can make an erection physically possible, but they cannot make you want sex. A lot of people who are let down by Viagra are let down precisely because their limiting factor was desire, not blood flow, and the drug was never going to address that.

Tadalafil for Women (yes it works!)

Female genital arousal, like a male erection, is partly a blood-flow event. Arousal in women depends on increased blood flow to the clitoris and vaginal tissue, which produces engorgement, more sensitivity, and lubrication. The clitoris and the penis come from the same tissue in development and share the same nitric-oxide-driven engorgement mechanism. So the same PDE5 inhibition that improves penile blood flow should, in theory, improve clitoral and vaginal blood flow. 

The strongest single piece of evidence is a study of premenopausal women with type 1 diabetes who had female sexual genital arousal disorder. Daily tadalafil at 5mg was measured two ways, subjectively through a validated sexual experiences questionnaire, and objectively through color Doppler ultrasound of clitoral blood flow. Tadalafil produced significant improvements in arousal, orgasm, enjoyment, satisfaction, and how often the women had sexual thoughts, along with measurable increases in clitoral blood flow and quality-of-life scores. The objective Doppler numbers matter here. This was not just self-report. The blood-flow improvement was physically measured. AlgoRX actually prescribes this one for women as well, which is a huge plus. 

Tadalafil's Two Dosing Strategies

Tadalafil is distinctive because of its long half-life (around 17.5 hours), which allows two different approaches. As-needed dosing (10 to 20mg taken before activity) gives a long window of effectiveness, up to 36 hours, which earned it the nickname the weekend pill. The other approach, low-dose daily dosing (2.5 to 5mg every day), keeps a steady low level in the system so sex can be spontaneous rather than planned around a pill, and this daily approach has an extra benefit. It is also FDA-approved to treat benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) symptoms, so it handles urinary symptoms at the same time. Many men prefer the daily low dose for the spontaneity and the dual benefit.

The two models:

As-needed: 10 to 20mg before anticipated activity. Long window (up to about 36 hours). Good for infrequent or plannable activity.

Daily low-dose: 2.5 to 5mg every day. Allows spontaneity, steady levels, and also treats BPH and urinary symptoms. Popular for regular activity.

Sildenafil (Viagra), by contrast, is shorter-acting (about a 4 to 6 hour window), taken as needed about 1 hour before, and is affected more by food. Tadalafil's length and daily option are its main advantages. This one can also be used more acutely as a pre-workout. 

Safety

The critical PDE5 safety rule: never combine PDE5 inhibitors with nitrates (nitroglycerin and other nitrate heart medications). The combination can cause a catastrophic, potentially fatal drop in blood pressure. 

Also use caution combining with alpha-blockers and other blood-pressure-lowering agents. 

Sudden ED can be an early sign of cardiovascular disease. If this happens, speak to a doctor.



Kisspeptin

Text saved

What it targets: Desire and arousal (central), plus the reproductive hormone axis.

Mechanism: Stimulates GnRH, which sits upstream of the entire sex-hormone system.

Evidence level: Promising, but investigational.

Best for: Low desire and HSDD.

Route: Injection (IV or SC) in trials.

How It Works

Kisspeptin is a naturally occurring hormone that sits at the top of the reproductive control system. It stimulates the release of GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) from the hypothalamus, which in turn drives the whole downstream cascade of reproductive hormones, LH, FSH, and ultimately testosterone and estrogen. This is the same HPG axis described in the hormone course, and kisspeptin is essentially the master switch that sits above it. But beyond its hormonal role, kisspeptin also appears to act directly on brain regions involved in sexual desire and attraction, and that is what makes it interesting for libido specifically.

The Evidence

The best data comes from Imperial College London. Two clinical trials published in JAMA Network Open studied kisspeptin in 32 premenopausal women and 32 men, all with hypoactive sexual desire disorder. Using functional MRI while participants viewed erotic material, the researchers found that kisspeptin improved sexual brain processing in both women and men, with positive effects on sexual behavior compared to placebo, and women reported feeling more sexy after receiving it. Importantly, the team had previously shown that kisspeptin boosts responses to sexual stimuli and attraction-related brain pathways independent of testosterone, meaning the desire effect is not just a downstream consequence of raising hormones.

A particularly notable practical finding: in women with HSDD, kisspeptin activated brain regions tied to sexual arousal and increased self-reported arousal without the nausea that limits PT-141. If that advantage holds up in larger trials, it could make kisspeptin a better-tolerated option than the current melanocortin approach. The researchers have described kisspeptin as potentially a safe and effective therapy for both women and men with distressing low desire, laying the foundation for kisspeptin-based HSDD treatments.

In my opinion, Kisspeptin is likely still best utilized when the issue is hormonally-driven issues with sexual desire and function. The one thing you want to avoid is using kisspeptin outside of that situation, and potentially altering your current hormonal situation in a way that you'll need to adjust for in the future.



Troches and Combination Formats

Text saved

A troche (pronounced troh-kee) is not a compound. It is a delivery format and one that you can commonly find a combination of these compounds in. This is another one that you can find easily with AlgoRX. 

What a Troche Is

A troche is a small, dissolvable lozenge meant to be held in the mouth, usually against the cheek or under the tongue, where it dissolves slowly and the active ingredients absorb directly through the tissues of the mouth (the oral mucosa) into the bloodstream. This sublingual or buccal absorption bypasses the digestive system and the liver's first-pass metabolism. For peptides and hormones used in sexual health, this direct absorption can mean faster onset and better bioavailability than swallowing a pill. 

Why They Are Popular for Sexual Health

Troches have become a favored format in sexual health for a few practical reasons. They are needle-free, which is a major appeal for compounds that would otherwise need injection (like PT-141 or oxytocin because the last thing you want to do is have to inject something every time you want to engage in these activities). A compounding pharmacy can also put several complementary compounds into a single troche, like PT-141, Oxytocin, and tadalafil all in one. And the onset is reasonably quick because of the direct mucosal absorption, which suits the as-needed, before-activity use pattern that most of these compounds follow.

Because sexual response has multiple components, combining compounds that target different components in a single troche is potentially even more beneficial. A common example is combining PT-141 (for desire) with oxytocin (for intimacy and orgasm quality), and sometimes a PDE5 inhibitor like tadalafil (for the blood-flow and erection component), a single troche that addresses desire, connection, and physical arousal at once. This is allowing you to hit multiple potential issues, or provide boosts in multiple different areas, all at once.



Libido Through Menopause

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/1fd606e3?md=74ca965c9b9d41e5bf02ff431b8470d1

Text saved

One important thing we need to address is libido during menopause as that is a MAJOR time where women may experience these issues. We have to acknowledge how libido changes through menopause and the broader hormonal transitions, because it is one of the most common real-world drivers of low desire, and it connects directly to the HRT course. For a huge number of women, the low libido they are trying to address is not going to be solved with peptides.

Why Libido Changes at Menopause

As covered in the hormone course, menopause involves the decline of estrogen and progesterone, and, often overlooked, a decline in testosterone, which women produce and which contributes meaningfully to their libido, energy, and sense of wellbeing. These hormonal shifts affect sexual desire through several channels at once. Declining testosterone directly reduces libido, since testosterone is a key driver of desire in women just as in men. Declining estrogen causes genitourinary changes, like vaginal dryness, thinning of tissues, and discomfort or pain during sex, that make sex less comfortable and so less wanted. And the broader symptoms of the transition, like disrupted sleep, mood changes, hot flashes, and fatigue, all indirectly suppress desire. Low libido at menopause is generally caused by the combination of all of these things rather than something specific. 

Why This Matters 

If low desire is being driven by menopausal hormonal decline, the most logical first-line approach is often to address the hormones, not to reach immediately for a libido peptide. You're just trying to fix symptoms in that case vs fixing the root cause. 

Estrogen therapy (covered in the HRT course) addresses the genitourinary symptoms. Local vaginal estrogen is highly effective for the dryness and discomfort that make sex painful, and systemic estrogen helps the broader symptom picture. Removing pain and discomfort often restores desire that was being suppressed by them.

Testosterone therapy for women (also covered in the HRT course) directly targets the desire component. The strongest evidence for testosterone in women is specifically for low libido and HSDD in postmenopausal women, dosed to female physiologic levels. For many women, restoring testosterone is the single most effective intervention for menopausal low desire.

PT-141 can then be a complementary option. It works on desire centrally and independently of hormones, so it can be layered in for women whose desire stays low even after hormonal optimization, or used as an on-demand tool alongside hormonal therapy.

So the best approach for menopausal low libido is usually to address the hormonal foundation first (estrogen for comfort, testosterone for desire), and consider the libido peptides as complementary or for residual low desire, rather than jumping straight to a peptide while completely ignoring the underlying hormonal drivers.



Dapoxetine

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/1fd606e3?md=978cad3617fa484ebd9df5653011b79a

Text saved

What it targets: Ejaculatory control (premature ejaculation).

Mechanism: Short-acting SSRI. Raises serotonin at the synapse to delay ejaculation.

Best for: Premature ejaculation (PE) in men aged 18 to 64.

Typical dosing: 30mg or 60mg on demand, 1 to 3 hours before activity.

Route: Oral.

PT-141 handles desire, the PDE5 inhibitors handle erection, oxytocin handles intimacy and orgasm quality, but none of them touch ejaculatory control. And premature ejaculation is actually the single most common male sexual complaint, with global prevalence estimated somewhere between 20 and 40 percent. Dapoxetine is specifically targeting this and helps with something none of these other compounds do, which is the timing of ejaculation.

What It Is

Dapoxetine (brand name Priligy) is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, an SSRI. The difference is that it was designed for on-demand treatment of premature ejaculation instead of for depression. What makes it work for this is the pharmacokinetics. It is absorbed fast, with peak levels within 1 to 2 hours, and it has a very short half-life, around 1 to 1.5 hours. So unlike the usual SSRIs that you take daily and build up over weeks, dapoxetine gets taken only when needed, a few hours before sex, and then it clears out quickly.

How It Works

The mechanism ties back to a side effect everyone already knows about with SSRIs. Serotonin plays an inhibitory role in ejaculation, so more serotonin signaling in the relevant nerve pathways delays it. That is why the SSRIs used for depression can cause delayed ejaculation and trouble reaching orgasm as a side effect. Dapoxetine takes advantage of that side effect and intentionally tries to target it to get results. By blocking serotonin reuptake, it raises serotonin at the synapses of the neurons that control ejaculation, which raises the threshold and delays the reflex.

Dosing

Start at 30mg, taken 1 to 3 hours before anticipated activity.

Move up to 60mg if 30mg is not enough and is well tolerated. 60mg works better but comes with somewhat more side effects.

On-demand only, not daily.

Can be taken with or without food.

Side Effects

Dapoxetine's side effects are generally mild, and because of the on-demand short-acting use they tend to be more short-lived than with daily SSRIs.

Nausea: the most common one, dose-related, more common at 60mg.

Dizziness and headache: common.

Other SSRI effects: diarrhea, insomnia or drowsiness, and dry mouth can happen.

Safety:

Do not combine it with MAOI antidepressants, other SSRIs, or certain other serotonergic drugs..
