Read This First

Before you think about switching GLPs, I'm going to tell you not to do it.

Why? Because most of the time it's going to be harder than you expect and it's not the thing that you actually need.

People switch GLPs to break a stall when they really should be going through the breaking-a-stall course instead.

They do it because they heard another compound was better but they never assessed why they weren't getting results in the first place.

If you plan to switch GLPs then you need to understand that you will be hungry.

You need to understand that it may take months to see any sort of results.

Stacking compounds during the transition isn't going to do it for you either. You'll never know when the actual compound you're switching to would be working and you're going to spend more money than if you stayed on the other compound.

The only time it truly makes sense is because you have negative side effects and you're switching to a compound that has fewer of them.

I see more people struggle in this transition period than anything else. I see so many people try to switch back because they realized it wasn't what they wanted and they weren't prepared for what was going to happen and what it was going to be like.

Overview

The goal of this course is to discuss transitions between GLPs and what happens when you move from one to another, why you might need to, what your body does in response to this, and how to manage this process appropriately.

The course is going to cover three transition directions - semaglutide to tirzepatide, tirzepatide to retatrutide, and semaglutide to retatrutide. Each of them have their own receptor mechanics, experiences, and practical considerations.

By the time you finish this, you should understand where and why transitions may be the most applicable, what to expect, how to recognize whether a transition is working, and how to manage the challenges that come along with it.

Community Questions

Can I stack a GLP + Cagrilintide during the transition? I am against doing this. One of the reasons is that you don't even know how you will respond to the GLP that you're switching to until you do it. If you immediately start by stacking another compound in there, you have no ability to actually assess the effectiveness of the new compound. In the course I tell you to ensure that you are mentally prepared for the transition and for things like this to happen. Mentally you should be ready and not feel the need or requirement to use Cagrilintide.

Can / should I taper off of one while adding in another? Similar to the answer to the first question, I would not taper off of one while switching to another. Again you want to observe how your body was responding to that compound over time and the biggest issue is starting at a low dosage more than anything else. Over time those issues that you would feel that make you feel like you need to use both compounds at the same time will eventually go away.

Should I take something in the transition period? I personally would not intentionally try to take something during the transition period just so you're not skewing your body's ability to perceive what is actually happening with your system. If you have a desire to use something else during that period to keep body fat down, it's honestly not going to be so effective because it's going to be a completely different compound.

One thing during this is the mental preparation that it takes to be okay with feeling different during the transition period and knowing that it is only temporary and will eventually return back to how you felt before. It just might take time.

Should I switch because everyone is talking about this other compound? Just because people are saying that a compound is so great doesn't mean that it's so much better that it justifies complications or the headache of going through a transition and having to learn how your body will respond to a new compound. If you go through the decision-making process within this course, it will tell you the things to consider, which is loss of progress or side effect mitigation. Those should be the primary drivers of a transition as opposed to thinking there are all of these crazy benefits before actually checking the data to see how significant those differences are between the compounds.

Why might a switch not work? For one everybody is different and certain things do not work for them the way that their body needs it to even if it works for others. The way that a compound is biased on receptors or the receptors at a target just might not be the right fit.

Now outside of that the anecdotal evidence that we're seeing is that people are ultimately just not patient enough and not willing to go through the mental and often times emotional requirements and demands that a transition takes. Typically people don't feel the way they want to, are unable to mentally manage the transition, and they end up feeling like they have to go back to the previous compound.

When does it make sense to switch? The decision-making process in this course outlines the best justifiable reasons, which is a stall in progress that has not been broken by other factors or in order to mitigate side effect profiles that may be lesser on another compound. Now if you just have a desire to switch and are mentally prepared for what you are going to go through, I think that is justifiable without a legitimately strong reasoning.

If you were a slow / slower responder to one GLP, will it make you a better candidate for a different one? If one compound didn't work well for you for various reasons, it really depends on what compounds you're switching to and what the reason was why you maybe didn't respond well. At the end of the day they are new compounds and so there is a good chance that a new compound that targets different receptors will work better for you.

Should I switch to get the extra benefits of Retatrutide? It's really going to depend on what the benefit actually is.

  • If you're referring to mitigating muscle loss, at the end of the day eating the appropriate calories and using a lesser dose of a GLP, if needed to do so, will help mitigate muscle loss.

  • If you're looking at visceral fat reduction, all GLPs, as well as weight loss in general, will help with this.

  • If you're looking at fat burning, getting a little bit of extra movement in and increasing your overall caloric expenditure will likely offer you that benefit.

Look at what the actual benefit is and figure out how much more significant that benefit may be with Retatrutide. Is there another way I can do that that justifies not going through the process of switching?
