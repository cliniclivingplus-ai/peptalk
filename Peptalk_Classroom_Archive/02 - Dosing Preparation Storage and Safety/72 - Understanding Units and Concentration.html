Overview

This course will break down what a unit is, what concentration is, and how to understand that as it relates to dosages.

What Is a Unit?

When people are referring to units in regards to peptides, they are not referring to milligrams or micrograms (or they're mistakenly referring to them that way due to lack of understanding). They're talking about the volume of liquid that is being drawn up into the syringe.

So what exactly is one unit?

If you are using a standard 1ml insulin syringe, it will typically be marked with little dashes like on a ruler from 0-100. A .3ml insulin syringe will have 0-30. A .5ml insulin syringe will have 0-50.

  • 100 units = 1mL

  • 1 unit = 0.01mL

  • 10 units = .1mL

Let's just assume you have a peptide vial that you have already reconstituted. If you draw up 10 units on the syringe, you are drawing out .1mL of the liquid. How much actual peptide that's in that .1mL is going to depend on the concentration of that liquid in the vial.

What Is Concentration?

Concentration basically tells you how much peptide is in each mL of liquid once it is mixed.

I like to compare this to adding sugar to tea:

  • The more sugar you add to the tea, the sweeter it will be.

  • The same with peptides. The more mg per mL, the stronger the dose will be per spray or injection in the same volume of liquid.

How do you calculate this concentration?

  • Peptide amount (in mg) / bac water (in mL) = Concentration (mg/mL)

  • For example:

    • You add 2ml of bac water to a 5mg vial.

    • 5mg / 2mL = 2.5 mg/mL.

    • Every 1mL of liquid contains 2.5mg of the peptide.

How does this correlate to units?

  • If your concentration is 2.5 mg/mL and your injection is .1 mL:

    • 0.1 mL x 2.5 mg/mL = .25mg or 250 mcg

Understanding Dose Based on Concentration

Once you know the concentration, you are then able to calculate the units based on the dosage.

Lets say that you reconstituted a 5mg vial with 2.5mL of liquid. That is going to give you a concentration of 2 mg per mL. You need to determine how much liquid is going to give you .5 mg (your dosage).

  • .5mg / 2 mg / mL = 0.25 mL

Since 1 mL = 100 units, then:

  • 0.25 mL = 25 units

You can also use a peptide calculator to assist with this, but this is explaining the concept behind it.

The most important formula to remember:

  • Volume to inject = Desired dose / Concentration

To determine the units based on volume, you need to remember this:

  • Since 100 units = 1 mL, multiple the mL amount by 100 to determine the units

    • 0.1 mL = 10 units

    • 0.2 mL = 20 units

    • 0.25 mL = 25 units

    • 0.5 mL = 50 units
