Text saved

The purpose of this category is to outline different peptides, common vial size amounts, and the simplest ways to reconstitute based on the dosage and vial size. 

The goal with each of these is to make the dosage AT LEAST 5 units on the low side to make it easier to draw and administer. This takes out the issues with a peptide calculator where it might tell you the dosage needed is 2 units and it becomes difficult to draw and/or administer. Each page will be broken down by peptide. 

DISCLAIMER: This should be used as a general guide. This is not medical advice and you should always consult with your doctor or provider. This is not meant to replace anything from your medical provider and is meant to serve as a reference guide to help with easier understanding.

AOD-9604

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/e13cb301?md=d54cf8da38ac4490b66bef891e1dc5e4

Text saved

Common vial sizes for AOD-9604 include 5mg and 10mg vials. 

Reconstitution by vial size-

5mg vial:

Add 1.6ml (160 units) of bacteriostatic water and .4ml (40 units) of acetic acid solution OR add 2.5ml (250 units) of bacteriostatic water and .5ml (50 units) of acetic acid solution. 

Common dosages based on these reconstitution amounts: 

2ml of liquid (1.6ml/.4ml):

250mcg (.25mg)- 10 units

300mcg (.3mg)- 12 units

500mcg (.5mg)- 20 units

3ml of liquid (2.5ml/.5ml) (preferred): 

250mcg (.25mg)- 15 units

300mcg (.3mg)- 18 units

500mcg (.5mg)- 30 units

10mg vial: 

Add 2.5ml (250 units) of bacteriostatic water and .5ml (50 units) of acetic acid solution. 

Common dosages based on this reconstitution amount: 

250mcg (.25mg)- 7.5 units

300mcg (.3mg)- 9 units

500mcg (.5mg)- 15 units




NOTE: AOD has a tendency to gel. Using bacteriostatic water and acetic acid solution can mitigate this.
