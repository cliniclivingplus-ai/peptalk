Understanding Cognitive Function

One of the toughest questions I get is "what is good for cognitive function"? It's tough because cognitive function is really a broad range of things. Anywhere from memory, learning, attention, problem-solving, etc. There are a lot of things that go on in the brain that can allow these things to occur. 

Neurotransmitters: These are chemical messengers that transmit signals between neurons that are fundamental to cognition. Glutamate is the primary excitatory neurotransmitter and plays a role in learning and memory, where acetylcholine is involved in memory formation and learning. Dopamine can modulate motivation, focus, and reward-based learning behaviors. You really need to have the right balance between the excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmitters to have clear thinking, attention, and memory. Imbalances with these neurotransmitter systems can cause cognitive impairments like Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, ADHD, etc. 

Neurogenesis: This is the process of generating new neurons. For adults, neurogenesis is thought to contribute to learning and memory formation. If you're able to enhance neurogenesis, then you can improve cognitive resilience. You can do this through things like increasing brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF). 

Synaptic Plasticity: Cognitive function depends a lot on your brain's ability to strengthen and form new synaptic connections which is known as synaptic plasticity. Long-term potentiation is sustained strengthening of synapses that allows for learning and memory storage. If you lose synaptic connections or your brain isn't able to adapt, it can impair memory. Neurotrophic factors such as BDNF and nerve growth factor (NGF) can support synaptic plasticity. 

Inflammation and Oxidative Stress: Neuroinflammation, which is the activation of immune cells in the release of inflammatory cytokines, can be a double-edged sword. Acute inflammatory responses can help to aid in injury repair, but chronic inflammation in the brain can cause damage to neurons and synapses which an lead to cognitive impairment and neurodegeneration. Oxidative stress (excess free radicals) can injure brain cells and impair cognitive function like memory. Elevated inflammatory markers and oxidative damage are observed in many neurodegenerative diseases. Reducing this is important to preserving cognition. 

Neurodegeneration: Over the course of your life, your neurons can become dysfunctional or die. Neurodegeneration often involves excitotoxicity (neuronal damage from excess glutamate signaling), mitochondrial dysfunction, and chronic inflammation. Protecting neurons and clearing / preventing toxic protein aggregates are the key to prevent cognitive decline in neurodegenerative diseases. 

So how can peptides help in these areas? 

Some peptides can stimulate growth factors in the brain, which will promote neurogenesis and synaptic repair. Some peptides can increase BDNF and NGF expression which can lead to the growth of new neurons and synapses. 

Other peptides are neuroprotective and inti-inflammatory which helps to protect neurons from oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokines. Doing so can create a better environment for neurons to survive and function. 

A lot of the cognitive peptides can modulate neurotransmitter systems. Some of them can enhance the cholinergic transmission, which is important for memory and attention, and others can modulate dopamine and / or serotonin, improving mood, motivation, and focus. 

Some peptides show the potential to reduce neurodegenerative pathology. Certain peptides can decrease beta-amyloid plaque accumulation (misfolded proteins that form in the space between nerve cells) and tau phosphorylation and can slow the progression of neurodegeneration or cognitive decline.

So what is Cerebrolysin? 

Cerebrolysin is a mixture of peptides that comes from big brain proteins and has pieces that are analogous to natural neurotrophic factors like brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), nerve growth factor (NGF), glial cell line-derived factor (GDNF). It was developed in Europe and has been used for a long time as a neuroprotective / nootropic compound. It is primarily administered through intramuscular or intravenous injection. 

What can it help with? 

Neurotrophic Support: Cerebrolysin can activate signaling pathways that are associated with growth and survival of neurons. It can upregulate endogenous neurotrophic factors like BDNF, NGF, and VEGF in the brain, resulting in the promotion of neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity. 

Neuroprotection: Cerebrolysin has antioxidant effects and reduces neuroinflammation and can downregulate inflammatory cytokines like TNF-a in the brain and can mitigate excitotoxic damage by modulating glutamate receptors and calcium influx, reducing neuron death during ischemic or toxic stress. 

Preventing Neurodegeneration: In preclinical Alzheimer's models, Cerebrolysin inhibited the formation of beta-amyloid plaques and tau tangles which are the protein aggregates that drive neurodegeneration. This is one of the things that separates it from other nootropics that are mostly targeting symptoms, and may actually enhance the clearance of the proteins or protecting the neurons from their toxic effects. 

Metabolic Enhancements: Cerebrolysin can also help with improving cerebral metabolism and perfusion by enhancing glucose uptake and mitochondrial function in neurons and can help brain cells produce energy more efficiently, supporting overall cognitive processing. 

What is the cognitive impact of Cerebrolysin? It has shown benefits in a lot of conditions of cognitive impairment, especially those related to neurodegenerative of vascular diseases. Some of the impacts include: 

Overall improvements in cognitive function like memory, attention, and daily functioning. This was seen in multiple small trials for patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease or vascular dementia. 

In one trial, Cerebrolysin plus standard therapy improved memory performance in older adults who had memory complaints. In a study of patients with schizophrenia patients, Cerebrolysin led to better cognitive test scores compared to controls. 

Cerebrolysin is also used for acute neurological injuries like strokes or TBI to aid in recovery. They actually did a large trial in 2012 where post-hoc analyses suggested that severe stroke patients might benefit from the use of Cerebrolysin. They also observe more consistently that in rehabilitation phases after a stroke or TBI, that Cerebrolysin can speed up functional recovery and improve cognitive outcomes like memory and attention. 

One important thing to note about Cerebrolysin is that it gives memory and cognitive benefits, particularly in those with deficits, but it is not a stimulant. It is going to gradually support better cognitive function through brain repair and optimization.

P21

P21 is a synthetic tetra-peptide that comes from the active piece of CTNF, which is a large neurotrophic protein that can't cross the blood-brain barrier, so they focused on this active piece of it in order to leverage its benefits. The goal of P21 was to be used as a therapy for things like Alzheimer's or other neurodegenerative conditions. 

What can it help with? 

BDNF Upregulation: P21 can significantly increase levels of BDNF in the brain, which is highly important for neuron survival, growth of new neurons, and formation of neural synapses. This can lead to increases neurogenesis and they even saw the birth of new neurons in animal studies that matured into functional cells.  

Reduced Neurodegeneration: Similarly to Cerebrolysin, P21 can actually help to reduce the accumulation of toxic proteins that are associated with Alzheimer's. In mouse models, they saw Tau protein buildup reduced and a reduction in the b-amyloid plaques. It seems like it can either inhibit or reverse the process that can lead to the tangles and plaque formation. This is one of the major reasons it seems promising for Alzheimer's disease.  

Synaptic Repair and Plasticity: P21 can help to preserve and restore synapses in the brain. There are things called dendritic spines which are protrusions where synapses occur, and P21 maintained healthier spine counts and synaptic marker levels in animal models. By doing this, it can help to support memory and cognitive function. 

PAK Pathway Support: It seems like P21 might intersect with the P21-activated kinase (PAK) pathway in neurons. Alzheimer's disease is associated with defects in PAK signaling. So, by improving overall neuron health and possible engaging those related pathways, P21 might indirectly help to normalize PAK signaling to protect the dendritic spines (see previous bullet point). 

What is the cognitive impact of P21? It has shown a lot of very impressive cognitive benefits in preclinical studies. Some of the impacts include: 

In Alzheimer's mouse models, P21 treatment let to restored cognitive function. Mice that normally will develop memory deficits performed significantly better in maze tests and object recognition tasks. They remembered and learned almost at a normal level, where the untreated mice were impaired greatly. So, it not only halts some of the disease processes, but also helps restore the functional recovery of memory abilities. 

P21 also improved neurogenesis-linked cognition and was observed in doing so in middle-aged healthy rats. It enhanced hippocampal neurogenesis which correlated with improved learning in certain tasks. More newborn neurons helped with pattern separation and memory formation. 

One important thing with P21 is that no human trials have been published yet. However, the animal data that exists for it show that it could potentially have benefits for age-related cognitive decline and early Alzheimer's disease. People who have experimented with it have said they had better mental clarity, working memory, and sometimes even mood. 

One important thing to note about Cerebrolysin is that it gives memory and cognitive benefits, particularly in those with deficits, but it is not a stimulant. It is going to gradually support better cognitive function through brain repair and optimization.
