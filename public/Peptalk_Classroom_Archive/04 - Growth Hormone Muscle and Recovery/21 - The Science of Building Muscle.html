Overview

People ask me all the time what they can take to build muscle. Before we get into any compounds, I want to explain what your body is actually doing when it builds muscle and what you can do to support that process.

We’ll start with what happens inside a muscle fiber, then get into the things you can control: how you train, how hard your sets are, how much protein you need, and how recovery affects your progress. We’ll also cover losing fat while keeping muscle, which is especially relevant for anyone using a GLP-1.

Later in the course we’ll look at creatine, testosterone, anabolic steroids, growth hormone, and peptides.

This course is educational and is not individualized medical advice. If you have a health condition, injury, or questions about medication, work with your healthcare professional.

Steroids, Peptides, GH, and Supplements

Some compounds can substantially increase muscle. Others not so much. The most important things to actually increase muscle are the things that were mentioned prior to this section in the course.

Testosterone and anabolic steroids

Anabolic steroids can directly increase muscle size and strength. In a 10-week randomized trial, men given a high, supraphysiologic dose of testosterone increased muscle size even without lifting. The group that received testosterone and trained gained more than both. As you can see here, training and nutrition were STILL the most important pieces of this.

You would be surprised at the amount of people taking steroids that you would never guess. Many have underwhelming results from these as they don't do it themselves and won't outperform the basics.

Growth hormone

Growth hormone can increase lean body mass in some capacity, but lean mass includes water and other tissue. A review of studies in healthy young adults found that GH did not improve muscle strength over the weeks to months studied. This is relatively overrated for muscle growth and is better used for sleep, recovery, and moderate fat loss.

Peptides

The GH peptides are the most common that are discussed when it pertains to this. These ultimately fall into the same discussion we just had as it pertains to growth hormone.

Creatine and protein

Creatine monohydrate helps regenerate energy during short, demanding efforts. Combined with resistance training, studies suggest it can modestly improve lean-mass gains on average. A common daily amount is around 5 grams. Creatine can help, but isn't doing it all for you. Protein powder is best used to assist you in reaching your total protein intake needed each day.
