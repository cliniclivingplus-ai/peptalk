A broken bone knits back together in a few weeks. A cut on your hand closes in days. But a bad tendon or a torn up knee can drag on for a year, and cartilage very often never fully heals at all.

These tissues are built in a way that makes them hard to repair, and until you understand that part, none of the treatment options make much sense.

How this course is built

Part 1 is the biology. What joints, cartilage, and tendons actually are, and why they heal so badly.

Part 2 is the tools. Loading and rehab, nutrition and supplements, the peptides, the injections, then surgery.

Part 3 is the plan. Turning all of it into something you can run for your injury.

This is education, not medical advice and not a treatment plan. Joint and tendon problems need a real diagnosis. What people call a tendon problem can be a tear, a tendinopathy, referred pain from somewhere else, or something completely different, and the right treatment depends on which one it is.

Get imaging and a clinician who knows what they are looking at before you build a plan around any of this. Self treating an undiagnosed joint is how people make things permanently worse.
