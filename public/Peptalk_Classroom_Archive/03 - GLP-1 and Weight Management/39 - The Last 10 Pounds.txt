Almost everybody who has lost a decent amount of weight describes the same experience where the first bit comes off pretty easily and predictably. Then it might slow down or fluctuate throughout the middle. Near the end the whole thing basically stops and that last 5, 10, or even 15 lbs. is completely different than everything that came before. Everything that has been working for you now doesn't and your scale is basically stalled for weeks. You start to get frustrated with actually being able to successfully get that weight off. 

This course is meant to be a troubleshooting guide but with a very specific focus. You could look at it as a companion course to the Breaking a Stall course within the community not necessarily a replacement. If your weight has stalled and you want the full general-purpose step-by-step protocol through any plateau at any point in the journey then that course would be the primary resource. 

What the goal of this course is adding is the specificity about the end of the journey. The last 10 lbs. are really a different physiological and psychological situation. Your body is going to defend its weight the hardest when you're already lean and your margin of error gets significantly smaller. There's honestly a question that may come up here, which is: is this last bit even worth chasing?

I think that this page is one of the most important sections and most people probably won't tell you that this is the reality. Sometimes the healthiest wisest thing to do when you're trying to answer the question of "How do I lose the last 10 lbs?" is you don't. You just shift to maintaining and you call that success. Knowing when to stop is important and it may be a healthier decision for you in the long run. 

There is a meaningful difference between losing weight for health and chasing a number for aesthetics or because that was a number that you set in your head in the beginning. If you're already at a healthy body weight and body fat level, the last 10 lbs. might represent something arbitrary more so than an actual need to reach it. It may cost more in your well-being than the result is worth. 

As covered throughout this course, the final little bits require progressively more precision, restriction, and management of hunger and stress for progressively smaller and slower results. This is exactly the part where disorder patterns start to take root, where you have this obsessive tracking and extreme restriction and over-exercising and anxiety around food. This is more of a negative relationship that can damage the quality of life that you're living.

For many people grinding towards that last 10 lbs., it costs them sleep, social life, mental peace, and sometimes physical health in exchange for a marginal cosmetic change. That's honestly a bad trade and recognizing that isn't bad. 

So how do you know what situation you're in here? 

Are you already in a healthy weight and body fat range? If the answer is yes then that last 10 may not be something that you actually need to lose. 

Is the pursuit of that last 10 lbs. costing you your sleep, mood, social life, or your relationship with your food? If yes that cost may actually exceed the benefit. 

Are you showing any signs of disordered patterns like obsessive tracking or food anxiety or extreme restriction? If the answer is yes then stop and try to build a healthy relationship with food. 

Would maintaining where I am and being strong and healthy actually be a good outcome? For a lot of people the answer to this is probably yes.
