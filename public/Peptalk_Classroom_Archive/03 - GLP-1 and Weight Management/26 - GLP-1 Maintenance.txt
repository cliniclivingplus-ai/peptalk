This is a tough course. Why? Because GLP-1 maintenance looks different for everyone. There is no set answer on what is 100% guaranteed to be the right answer that works for you. Why is Maintenance Important?

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/9a1bbaca?md=c7e1e271086f4e7fb5ce0e14d7239ba3

Text saved

You've probably tried to diet before in your life. You lost some amount of weight and then gained it back again. That is incredibly common. Ultimately, without a continuance of the lifestyle and habits, that is almost bound to happen. GLP-1 medications can be incredibly beneficial for making this process easier. They help you with appetite control, they help your body release insulin more effectively and efficiently, and they support the effort that you put in to reach those goals. 

If you don't have a plan for maintenance, you could gain it all back when you lose those "boosts" that you get from the GLP-1s. People can successfully stop taking them (that's one of the options for maintenance), but it does take more work to do so. You've probably seen all of the information out there about people gaining the weight back after stopping a GLP-1. This is because they did not have a maintenance plan / strategy to keep their results, especially when hunger and cravings go back to where they were before.



What Maintenance Options Are There?

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/9a1bbaca?md=7c60ed55c8c4485da16bb1ffd2ec232e

Text saved

One thing that is important to understand is that this is going to be very individualized. In my opinion, nobody can tell you 100% which of these options is going to work for you with full certainty, or if you are going to be able to use the same approach forever. The reality is that you may start off with one of these options and change 6 months later. That is what makes maintenance a little bit more difficult. But, this is also what happens when starting the medication too. Some people respond very well at the lowest (or sometimes, lower than the typical lowest) dosages, and some have to work their way a little higher. Ultimately, we just need to understand how to look at these options conceptually, look at indicators of effectiveness, and understand how to approach this successfully. 

Continue to use the GLP-1 at the same dosage. This one doesn't seem like it's a "maintenance" option, but there are some who ultimately end up staying on 15mg of Tirzepatide because that's what works for them to maintain. Just know that it is an option although most people's goal seems to be to reduce their amount once they reach maintenance. 

Reduce the dosage and / or frequency. With this, you either reduce down the dosage you take or you reduce the frequency in which you take it. Sometimes, you might even reduce both where you're taking a lower dose at a lesser frequency than you were before. 

Discontinue the GLP-1 completely. Truthfully, this is probably the most difficult of all of the options as you no longer have the benefits of the medication within your system. Understand that this is not always an option for everyone to do depending on their own personal situation. If you choose to do this, it is the same as any other diet or lifestyle change. You HAVE to maintain the lifestyle and habits. The kicker is you no longer have the GLP supporting you in doing so, which makes it just a bit more difficult, but not impossible.



Option 1: Stay Where You're At

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/9a1bbaca?md=616d1536e2f3414ea4f08108e732250c

Text saved

This one may just fall into place naturally for you. We all know that while using these your body may begin to adapt to how you feel in terms of appetite suppression and other effects. We also know that over time, the rate of weight loss may slow. And for those who have gotten to, or are at this point, we know that the last 10 lbs always seem to be the most stubborn. So, this option may almost "fall into place" for you. 

As you start to near your goal weight, you might realize that the dose you're on almost turns into maintenance. This is why I say that this option may almost seem to fall into place for you. Once you reach that point, you may realize that the dosage that you're using keeps everything in check the way that it needs to be, whether it's appetite, A1C, etc. 

What are some of the reasons we might look at to decide if staying on the same dosage is the right answer outside of it "falling into place?" 

You've had issues regaining before. Maybe you lowered your dose, maybe you hit a stall and started gaining, etc. These could be indicators that staying at the same dosage might be the best option. There's also a component of this that plays into the second reason which is health conditions that cause reason number 1 to be more likely. 

You have other medical conditions that benefit from the higher dosages. This could be anything from diabetes, PCOS, fatty liver disease, blood pressure issues, cholesterol, etc. If there is a medical condition that is being controlled by using a higher dosage, then typically it makes more sense to stay there as opposed to titrating back down or coming off. Now, in certain scenarios, these issues may be fixed and allow for a dosage reduction once maintenance is reached. 

Everything feels good where you're at. Sometimes, you are tolerating a dosage well and everything about where you are just feels right. You have just the right amount of control over appetite and food noise and there's really no reason to change. You may be able to test the waters and see what reducing dosage or frequency might feel like, but where you're at just seems to feel right. In this case, there's really not a need to change things up. 

In any of these scenarios, staying where you are might be the right answer. Maybe you're not ready to titrate down. That's okay. But, eventually, maybe you will be. These are the things that I would evaluate to determine if staying at the same dosage is the right move.



Option 2: Reduce Dosage and / or Frequency

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/9a1bbaca?md=cac359c8b8f14e2895cf0f9d5e9e4388

Text saved

This is probably the most common option that people choose when they reach maintenance. Typically, people will continue to lose (regardless of how slow those last 10lbs might seem to be!) until they reach the point where they reach their goal. Once they reach that goal, they typically will continue to lose unless they make some type of adjustment. This is where it become a bit tricky. What is the adjustment we need to make, and how big of an adjustment do we need to make? There's really 3 ways to go about this, considering we can reduce dose, frequency, or both. Let's look at the ways that might make sense for this to be done:

Reducing frequency. This may be the easiest thing to start with. The reason being is that you can almost immediately gauge how you are going to respond to is as you can track things like hunger, satiety, food noise, etc. as the days go on. The easiest way to approach this in a gentle manner is going to be to increase the time between injections by one day each week until you find the sweet spot for it. At some point you will likely reach the point where you say "yep, this is the frequency that works for me." Now, it might be every 10 days, every 14 days, etc. The reality is that the frequency of time between dosages doesn't really matter much, as long as it's the frequency that works for you. Some people may choose to go straight to every 10 days vs. every 7, but I like the slow and steady approach to it just in case that seems to be too big of a jump to start with. It saves some headache of trying to figure out where the sweet spot might actually be. Here's a good study that talks about the efficacy of using a reduced frequency dosing with GLP-1 RAs https://dom-pubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dom.16229. 

Reducing the dosage. I said that reducing the frequency is probably the easiest thing to start with. This can be pretty easy too in all reality. Now, the same way that we went with a gentler approach to reducing frequency by going slowly can also apply here. Typically, a dosage schedule is a set range that jumps by the same amount each week as we increase dosages. For tirzepatide, it's 2.5mg typically. So, 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5, 15. That doesn't HAVE to be that way on the way up, and it doesn't HAVE to be that way on the way down. There's nothing to say that the dosage can't be reduced by a half-dosage on the way down just to make it a little less significant of a jump in case it's too much. So, it could be reduced by 1.25mg at first to make sure it's not too much. There's nothing wrong with reducing by a full-dose either though. But, if you're nervous about it not being sufficient for appetite control, etc, then the half-dose reduction is a little "safer" of a bet. Ultimately, you continue to reduce down the dosage until you finally reach the one that ends up being the amount that works best assist you in staying at your goal weight. Here's an article on tapering that talks about some observations that were made for people using this method https://easo.org/is-coming-off-semaglutide-slowly-the-key-to-preventing-weight-regain/. 

Reducing both. This is where it might start to get a little more "tricky" because now you're playing with two variables. Maybe reducing the dosage is the right answer for you or maybe it's the frequency. But can you do both? That's the unknown when it comes to doing both of these options together. However, the easiest approach is going to be reducing one at a time. So, let's say you cut your dosage down by one step. So, you go from something like 7.5mg of Tirzepatide to 5mg of Tirzepatide, or from 6mg of Retatrutide to 4mg of Retatrutide. You do this for a few weeks and you realize that you're still losing weight but maybe at a slower rate. At that point, you may decide to drop down another step on the dosage schedule, or you might try to extend your frequency by a day or so. You can essentially play with these two variables until you find the point that works for best for you. 

At some point during this process, you may realize you have to go up or go back to a higher frequency. Ultimately, this process is going to be a learning process where you have to continually monitor how your body is feeling and responding to figure out what is best long term. It may be one thing one day, and another the next. There is nothing that states that one approach is going to be the only approach for the rest of the time you're on the medications. And there is nothing to say that you can't come off in the long run after using these types of methods for maintenance.



Option 3: Stop Completely

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/9a1bbaca?md=3b570f7c2d414e5d9b4bd130405f8b53

Text saved

This is probably the most daunting of all of the options when people think about maintenance. When people think about coming off of GLP-1s, they think about all of the times where they tried to lose weight before without success. They think about all of the food noise they had before. They think about everything that they have experienced over the past years of their life where they feel like they failed and the only thing that finally allowed them to success was the support of the GLP-1s. 

But here's the thing - the GLPs weren't the only thing that got you to this point! Every step that you took on a treadmill, every time you walked in the gym, every time you chose a healthier meal option, those were all things that YOU did to get you to where you are. The only difference is that you had the support of a GLP-1. Now, this is also the part that can make this option more difficult. You've been using something that aids your body in many ways to support this process and ultimately make it a little easier. You might not have had to exercise as much discipline in your eating because these compounds took the food noise away. It changed the way your body handled and responded to the food that you ate. So the reality is, you do lose that when you come off of the compounds. 

However, you may have also created a new set point for your body as long as the weight was lost gradually and not a 2-month loss and then try to quit type of thing. You've changed your relationship with food and your relationship with exercise. You understand all of these things a little more because you've spent time learning about these things as well as your body and understanding yourself a little more. All of this plays into it. It isn't impossible to quit GLP-1s, it's just a little tougher sometimes. 

GLP-1s have a long half-life. Which means they do take quite a while to get out of your system. So, even if you quit cold-turkey,  you still have some level of them in your system for potentially a month or longer depending on the dosage you were on. So, if you completely come off, those cravings and feelings might not come back in full swing for a month or so. 

But, the reality is, that you might have to do Option 1 or Option 2 before getting to the point that you can come off completely. This may be the best option to coming off completely. Again, not everyone will want to or be able to do this for various reasons. But if you continue to reduce your dosage and frequency over time, you may find that you can come off completely and no longer need them. 

The most important piece here is remaining committed to lifestyle changes. There will be a level of personal discipline where you have to tell yourself that you don't need the cookies before bed and instead opt for Greek Yogurt with some berries. You have to tell yourself you will remain committed to physical activity. If you come off of the medications completely and lose all discipline, it's the exact same scenario as every other time you might have tried to "diet" the weight off and regained it.



Importance of Lifestyle

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/9a1bbaca?md=d77bc8609f6844b4ac7f6b3fd88e2500

Text saved

No matter which of the previous options you end up choosing, maintaining a healthy lifestyle that is committed to the right practices is the most important thing you are going to do. This doesn't mean you have to eat chicken and rice 4x a day for the rest of your life. But diet, physical activity, sleep, and managing your stress is going to support your weight goals for the rest of your life. I touch on some of this stuff in the nutrition and exercise courses, but let's go over some important things you can do to help maintain your results. 

Nutrition: 

Focus on getting in your protein. This is going to be just as important in maintenance as it was in your weight loss journey. It helps support lean muscle tissue and for a lot of people, eating higher protein can help with appetite control. Also, maintaining muscle tissue can help you burn more calories which will also help keep the weight off. 

Get fiber-rich foods. Fiber-rich foods can also help with a fullness feeling and allow you to have that fullness with fewer calories and can also help with stabilizing blood sugar (so great for those who had issues with this prior to GLPs). They also can help to slow digestion, which is a benefit that you were getting from the GLPs. So in a way, you're naturally adding something that gives you a similar (albeit maybe a less significant) ability to slow gastric emptying. You'll also want to ensure you're getting in plenty of water along with your fiber intake. 

Focus on portion control. An important thing here is to NOT overly restrict yourself thinking that it is needed when you come off of a GLP. There's some more details about this in the nutrition course, but eat your meals slowly. Focus on eating the meal when you sit down to eat and avoid distractions. This can help your body focus on the process of eating and help cue fullness. Also, if you're someone who eats when bored or stress eats, find a way to shift your mindset in those situations, or choose a higher volume, lower calorie snack. And if you have to, have you a piece of dark chocolate at night to help the sweet tooth. There's nothing wrong with that if it helps you not binge out on sweets. 

Exercise: 

Continue to do cardio and resistance training. As mentioned before, a higher level of muscle mass will also help your body continuously burn more calories. This can help mitigate a decrease in metabolism that may contribute to weight gain. Continue to do cardio and really try to walk 10-minutes after every meal. This helps with insulin sensitivity, digestion, and allows you to easily add in some extra calorie expenditure to your day. 

If you look in the training and exercise course, there's also a section that discusses NEAT (non-exercise activity thermogenesis). This is another CRITICAL piece of increasing caloric expenditure throughout the day. This is ways that you burn calories through various movement throughout the day. Things like walking up and down stairs, doing work around the house, walking the dogs, etc. This is severely overlooked as most think their daily workouts are burning all of the calories, but this does more than you realize. 

Accountability: 

Have some type of accountability plan for yourself. Continue to do weigh-ins and body composition scans if you were doing that before. Don't freak out if the scale fluctuates up and down a tiny bit, but focus on the trends. Look at how your clothes continue to fit. If you notice something moving in the wrong direction, make some adjustments to your lifestyle, nutrition, exercise, etc. 

Find a community to support you. The good news is, you're already a part of one here! But also, find some friends in your local area, find a workout buddy, find a support group, find someone who will support you through this, listen and give an outside perspective, and be there for you as things change. This is incredibly important because so many people feel like they go through all of this on their own and this really can make a huge difference.
