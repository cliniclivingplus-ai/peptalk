Energy is the thing people ask me about more than almost anything else. 

The problem is that "I'm tired" isn't one thing. It has about twenty different causes, and the right fix depends entirely on which one is actually yours. That's why most people never fix it. They grab another coffee or an energy drink and treat the symptom without ever finding out what's driving it.

This course is going to fix that.

We're going to start with what energy actually is, all the way down to the molecule your body runs on (ATP). Then the nine systems that produce and regulate it, because when you're tired, one or more of those systems is the reason. Then everything that drains you, which is the diagnostic part of this. Then we're going to go through everything that actually fixes it.

One idea runs through this whole course:

There's a difference between BORROWING energy and BUILDING it.

Caffeine and stimulants borrow. They take energy from later today and hand it to you now, and you pay it back with interest. Fixing your sleep, your mitochondria, your hormones, or a deficiency builds energy that you actually own.

Both have a place. But confusing the two is exactly why so many people are wired and exhausted at the same time.

How this course is laid out

Part 1 - What energy is: the biology, ATP, and how food and oxygen become power.

Part 2 - The systems: mitochondria, thyroid, adrenals, blood sugar, oxygen delivery, breathing, the gut, circadian rhythm, and the nervous system.

Part 3 - What drains you: the deficiencies, the medical causes, and the everyday stuff that's quietly wrecking you.

Part 4 - The fix catalog: sleep, food, movement, light, breathing, caffeine, supplements, hormones, peptides, and the mental side of energy.

Part 5 - Putting it together: figuring out which one is YOUR problem and building a plan around it.

The evidence tiers

Everything in here has an evidence tier that correlates to the data we have to support it. 

Tier 1 - Proven. The basics that unquestionably drive energy.

Tier 2 - Strong. Good evidence for real benefit. Worth doing for most people.

Tier 3 - Promising. Real mechanism and encouraging data, but not fully proven.

Tier 4 - Experimental or situational. Thin data or niche use.

Put your effort into the low numbers first. 

Before we start

This course is educational. It's not medical advice. Being tired all the time can be the first sign of something real, like anemia, thyroid disease, sleep apnea, or diabetes. Those need to be monitored and evaluated appropriately. 

If your fatigue is significant, new, or getting worse, skip ahead to Part 3 first. I list the labs to ask for and the red flags that mean go see somebody now.

The most important thing I can tell you is this: chronic tiredness almost always has a cause you can find and fix. Don't just caffeinate over it.
