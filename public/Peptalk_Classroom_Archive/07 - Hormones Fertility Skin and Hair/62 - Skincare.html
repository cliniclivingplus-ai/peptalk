Skincare Basics

This section will give you an overview of how peptides might interact with the skin and why certain peptides can be important for your skincare routine.

First, let's address what the skin does for you outside of just looking pretty.

  • Temperature regulation

  • Pathogen Defense

  • Nutrient Exchange

  • Immune System and Nervous System Communication

When your skin has issues, it can cause issues across your body.

There are 3 layers of the skin that play into all of this.

  • Epidermis- This is the outer layer of the skin. It's what we see when we look in the mirror. It helps with protection, cell turnover, and pigmentation. Most of the topical peptides are going to target this layer of the skin (think SNAP-8, topical GHK-Cu, etc).

  • Dermis- This layer is just below the epidermis and is where collagen and elastin are located. You also have things like blood vessels, hair follicles, and nerve endings in this layer. It helps with your skin's structure and firmness. When targeting skin remodeling, such as wrinkles and elasticity, this is really the layer that needs to be targeted for maximum results. Microneedling and injectables can target this layer more than topicals alone will.

  • Hypodermis- this is the deepest layer and is mostly fatty and connective tissue. Think of it more like an insulation / cushioning layer of the skin. This may play a role when you look at deep scars and stretch marks.

When we think about all of this, what does our skin truly need to be resilient?

  • Collagen and elastin- these help give your skin firmness and structure. Over time, these will decline with age and peptides can help stimulate their production and repair.

  • Inflammation control- This is super important as too much inflammation can lead to faster aging, redness, and irritation.

  • Hydration- This is critically underrated when it comes to skin health. This is super important to maintaining a strong skin barrier and mitigating issues like inflammation or premature wrinkles.

  • Cell turnover- This helps to keep the surface of your skin smoother, brighter, and functioning how it's supposed to. If cells are able to build up, then it can begin to dull your appearance.

Peptides, when used correctly, can help support all of these issues in some capacity alongside of a dedicated routine.

So, what can peptides help to improve?

  • Wrinkles and fine lines

  • Hyperpigmentation and unevenness

  • Acne and acne scarring

  • Stretch marks

  • Rosacea and redness

  • UV damage and oxidative stress

  • Poor skin recovery and wound healing

This sounds great! Seems like peptides can basically fix all of our skin issues! Well, not exactly. What can't they do?

  • Cleanse your skin. You still have to do this.

  • Hydrate. You need to drink plenty of water and fluids and eat nutrient-dense foods.

  • Sleep good and manage stress.

What Peptides Do for Skin

So, we discussed some of the basics of skincare, but how can peptides actually do things to help with our skin?

We already know that peptides are small chains of amino acids. They're going to tell your body to do specific things. When it comes to skincare, they can tell your body to produce more collagen, reduce inflammation, and speed up healing. With skincare, we can use them to target specific skin issues, often times with fewer side effects than more aggressive treatments (think KPV for acne vs. accutane).

How are these going to interact with your skin in order to provide benefits?

  • Tell your skin to produce more collagen or elastin

  • Reduce the release of pro-inflammatory molecules

  • Improve Skin barrier function

  • Assist with tissue repair

  • Inhibit muscle contraction

You can use them in different ways depending on how you need them to work.

  • Topical peptides will mostly stay in the top layer of the skin (epidermis) and can help with surface-level issues like texture, tone, and signs of aging.

  • Injectable or microneedling can go deeper into the dermis and can stimulate collagen production, rebuild structure, and fix some deeper issues like wrinkles and scarring.

    • When injected, they can also work systemically so they will help the skin overall by promoting healing and reducing inflammation.

What Does Each Peptide Do for Your Skin?

Here we will discuss what each peptide actually does for your skin. Not all of them are necessary, but understanding what and how each of them work can help you understand which may be best.

GHK-Cu

GHK-Cu is a copper peptide that can help stimulate collagen and elastin and help with skin firmness, skin repair, and reduce inflammation. It can also help even out issues with pigmentation like dark spots.

Best for: Anti-aging, wrinkles, dark spots, pigmentation, firmness, hair growth

How to use: Topical, microneedling, injectable

GHK Base (no Cu)

This is the same as GHK-Cu, just without the copper. It will still give most of the same benefits, but is a bit more mild and may be better for people who are sensitive to copper.

Best for: Anti-aging, wrinkles, dark spots, pigmentation, firmness, hair growth

How to use: Topical, microneedling, injectable

GHK-Biotin

This is a type of GHK that is bound to biotin and can have extra benefits for hair and nails. This is commonly used for things like hair growth, but may also help benefit skin.

Best for: Hair growth, skin regeneration

How to use: Topical, microneedling

SNAP-8

SNAP-8 is a Botox alternative that can reduce muscle contraction to soften wrinkles. It doesn't completely paralyze the muscle the way that Botox does, but can tone them down pretty significantly.

Best for: Wrinkles, expression lines

How to use: Topical

Argireline

Argireline works similarly to SNAP-8 and can help facial muscles relax to help reduce fine lines and wrinkles.

Best for: Wrinkles, expression lines

How to use: Topical

Leuphasyl

This works similarly on wrinkle pathways as Argireline, but slightly differently. It may enhance the muscle relaxing effect that is recognized by Argireline.

Best for: Deep wrinkles

How to use: Topical

Syn-Ake

Syn-Ake is an interesting compound as it mimics the activity of a snake venom, Waglerin-1, to help relax facial muscles and reduce the appearance of wrinkles.

Best for: Expression lines, wrinkles, smoothing

How to use: Topical

Matrixyl

Matrixyl can help stimulate collagen production and strengthen the skins extracellular matrix. It can help with hydration, elasticity, and firmness and may be good for thinning or crepey skin.

Best for: Firmness, hydration, skin texture

How to use: Topical, microneedling

Syn-Coll

Syn-Coll can help stimulate collagen synthesis and reduce the activity of enzymes that break down collagen, which can be great for people with sagging skin or lack of skin volume.

Best for: Tightening, collagen

How to use: Topical, microneedling

BPC-157

BPC-157 is a healing peptide that can help regenerate damaged skin and reduce inflammation. It can be beneficial for things like scarring and stretch marks.

Best for: Scarring, inflammation

How to use: Injectable, topical blends

KPV

KPV is a peptide with strong anti-inflammatory benefits that can help calm skin with issues like rosacea, eczema, psoriasis, and acne.

Best for: Redness, inflammation, eczema, psoriasis, acne

How to use: Injectable, topical

Melanotan

Melanotan stimulates melanin production to promote tanning and protect against UV damage.

Best for: Pigmentation, UV protection

How to use: Injectable, nasal

Zinc Thymulin

This is a combination of zinc and thymulin that can help regulate the skins immune function and is particularly useful for scalp and hair health, like hair regrowth and thickness.

Best for: Hair and scalp health

How to use: Topical, microneedling

Glutathione

Glutathione is a powerful antioxidant that can help detox the skin and lighten pigmentation. You will not turn into Casper, but it will help with skin clarity.

Best for: Pigmentation, antioxidant support

How to use: Injectable, topical

PDRN

PDRN is made from fragments of salmon DNA and it's all about repair. It helps kickstart your skin's own healing, boosts hydration, calms inflammation, and improves elasticity over time. This is a good one when skin is tired, depleted, or recovering from a procedure.

Best for: Repair, hydration, elasticity, plumping

How to use: Topical, microneedling, injectable

In render: PDRN Peptide Serum, PDRN Capsule Cream, PDRN Infinity Cream, PDRN Body Lotion, PDRN Mask

Exosomes

Exosomes are tiny messenger packets that carry growth signals between your skin cells. They basically tell your skin to repair and regenerate. A lot of people pair them with microneedling to push radiance and elasticity.

Best for: Fine lines, elasticity, radiance, recovery

How to use: Topical, microneedling

In render: Exosome Serum

NAD+

NAD+ is a coenzyme your cells use to make energy and run repair. Levels drop as we age, and tired cells tend to show up as tired skin. Topical NAD+ is about waking up dull skin and supporting renewal.

Best for: Tired skin, renewal, firmness

How to use: Topical

In render: NAD+ Serum

How to Apply for Best Results

So, we know what each peptide does. But how can we apply them to maximize results? If they aren't being used the right way, you may not see maximum results and are wasting money.

Topical Application

This is honestly the easiest way to use peptides. All you do is apply them to the skin like a serum or cream.

Peptides such as SNAP-8, Argireline, Leuphasyl, Syn-Ake, Matrixyl, GHK-Cu, and Syn-Coll are often found and used in topical formulations.

Things to remember:

  • Cleanse your skin. If you have dirty or oily skin, that is not good. WASH YO FACE!

  • Apply peptides first before using thicker moisturizers and oils so they can absorb. Preferably, space them out a bit from other products.

  • A thin layer is typically sufficient.

Topical products are mostly going to stay in the epidermis and are great for surface-level benefits like texture, hydration, and light wrinkle reduction. They can also help with the appearance of scars. They aren't going to be life-changing for deep scars and major structural issues.

Microneedling

Microneedling basically opens up little channels in the skin and lets the peptides go a little deeper to reach the dermis layer where the collagen and elastin actually are. They can increase the effectiveness of different peptides like GHK-Cu.

How to microneedle:

  • Use .25 to .5mm depth. The deeper depths are for deeper scarring and stretch marks and really should only be used if you truly know what you are doing.

  • Always use a clean and sanitized device. You wouldn't reuse dirty needles, so don't reuse dirty devices.

  • Apply the serum either in a hydropen, like the HydroDerma Pen, or immediately after using something like the DermTifix pen.

  • Avoid harsher ingredients like acids and retinoids for a couple of days after needling.

Microneedling can cause redness and sensitivity, so avoid long sun exposure, heavy workouts, etc immediately after.

Injectable Use

Some peptides will work best when injected and can give systemic benefits.

Peptides like GHK-Cu, BPC-157, KPV, Melanotan, and Glutathione are commonly used through this method of administration.

Injectables can improve skin from the inside out, help with deeper healing, scars, or widespread issues, support hair and scalp health.

Important things to remember:

  • Only inject peptides that are intended for subcutaneous or intramuscular use. They will be either lyophilized or already liquid and typically in a vial. The exception is SNAP-8. This may come in a vial. Don't inject it please.

  • Always use sterile practices.

  • Rotate injection sites.

  • Reconstitute with bacteriostatic water and store in the fridge.

Timing and Frequency

  • Topicals: 1-2x daily

  • Microneedling: Once every 1-2 weeks is generally sufficient.

  • Injectables: Depending on the peptide they may be daily, every other day, or 5 on 2 off.

Beyond Peptides: The Other Actives

Peptides get most of the attention, but they aren't the only actives that matter. The ones below aren't peptides. They handle hydration, brightness, texture, and protection, and a lot of them do the heavy lifting in a product. Knowing what they do is how you actually read an ingredient label instead of trusting the marketing. Where it fits, I've noted the render formula that already pairs them with the peptides from the last lesson.

Retinol (Vitamin A)

The most studied anti-aging active there is. It speeds up cell turnover, smooths texture, softens fine lines, and helps clear out pores. Start low and slow, a couple nights a week, and wear SPF because it makes your skin more sun-sensitive.

Best for: Fine lines, texture, overnight renewal

How to use: Topical, PM only

In render: Retinol & Collagen Capsule Cream

Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)

Your daytime antioxidant. It brightens, fades dark spots and uneven tone, supports collagen, and helps defend against pollution and UV stress. Use it in the morning under sunscreen.

Best for: Brightening, uneven tone, firmness, antioxidant defense

How to use: Topical, AM

In render: Vitamin C Infinity Cream, Vitamin C Body Lotion

Niacinamide (Vitamin B3)

The do-everything support active. It strengthens the barrier, controls oil, calms redness, evens tone, and helps with the look of pores. Very well tolerated and it stacks with almost anything.

Best for: Barrier, oil control, redness, tone

How to use: Topical, AM or PM

In render: Built into the capsule cream formulas as a support active

Hyaluronic Acid

A hydration magnet. It holds a lot of water to plump and smooth the surface, which is what gives skin that dewy look instead of a tight one. Put it on damp skin and seal it in with a cream.

Best for: Hydration, plumping, surface smoothing

How to use: Topical

In render: The hydrating gel base of the Capsule and Infinity Creams

Squalane

A lightweight, non-greasy emollient that mimics your skin's own oils. It softens, locks in moisture, and helps other actives absorb without clogging pores.

Best for: Softening, moisture retention, barrier support

How to use: Topical

In render: Blended into the capsule cream base with the peptides

Astaxanthin

One of the strongest antioxidants used in skincare. It's a red algae carotenoid that fights free-radical damage, supports a brighter and more even look, and helps skin hold up against everyday environmental stress.

Best for: Dullness, antioxidant defense, brightening

How to use: Topical

In render: Astaxanthin Capsule Cream

Centella Asiatica (Cica)

A calming, barrier-loving botanical. It soothes redness and sensitivity, supports healing, and reinforces the barrier. Good to pair with anything that can be irritating, like retinol or actives after microneedling.

Best for: Sensitivity, redness, barrier repair, soothing

How to use: Topical

In render: Centella Capsule Cream, Green Tea & Centella Asiatica Cleansing Mousse, and the gel base of every capsule cream

Salicylic Acid (BHA)

An oil-soluble exfoliant that gets into the pore to clear congestion, blackheads, and rough texture. This is the go-to for oily and acne-prone skin. Don't overdo it. A few times a week is plenty.

Best for: Congestion, pores, texture, acne

How to use: Topical, a few times per week

In render: Salicylic Acid Pads

Collagen (as a topical ingredient)

Topical collagen is a big molecule, so it mostly works on the surface by hydrating and smoothing. The peptides in the same formula, like Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1, are what actually signal your skin to make its own collagen.

Best for: Surface hydration and smoothing, paired with collagen-signaling peptides

How to use: Topical

In render: Retinol & Collagen Capsule Cream

Green Tea (EGCG)

A soothing antioxidant that calms and balances. It works well in a daily cleanser where it keeps skin from feeling stripped.

Best for: Daily cleanse, sensitivity, balance

How to use: Topical

In render: Green Tea & Centella Asiatica Cleansing Mousse
