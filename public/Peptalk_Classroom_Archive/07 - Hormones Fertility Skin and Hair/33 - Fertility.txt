Fertility is complex and has an underlying emotional component associated with it, as well as a lot of confusion. Couples trying to conceive get buried in advice, supplements, and expensive interventions, usually without a lot of understanding of what's actually happening in their bodies or what can truly make an impact. This course is meant to fix that and be a full map of the fertility for both men and women.

The approach is the same one that runs through the rest of the courses within the classroom, which is understanding the biology first and the interventions will start to make sense on their own. 

Fertility is a two-person equation and male factors contribute to roughly half of all infertility cases. Fertility still gets treated culturally as a woman's issue and men typically get skipped in the analysis of what might be wrong. That's why this course gives both sides a full discussion, a full men's section, and a full women's section, because optimizing fertility means optimizing both partners. The biology, the causes, and the treatments can be different for each. 

How this course is structured

Part 1 - The biology. How conception actually works, and the foundations both partners share.

Part 2 - Men's fertility. Sperm biology, causes, hCG/hMG/FSH and the medical therapies.

Part 3 - Women's fertility. The cycle and egg biology, causes, the workup, and the medical treatments including IVF.

Part 4 - The cross-cutting toolkit. Diet and lifestyle, supplements, and the peptides and bioregulators.

Part 5 - Putting it together. A couple's game plan and when to get help.

Fertility optimization is mostly about creating the healthiest possible environment for eggs and sperm. Both take about three months to develop, so the work you do now shows up in roughly three months, not overnight. Understanding that can help you get into the right mindset when you know that this process will take time.

Read this part before anything else

This course is educational. It's a map, not a treatment plan and not medical advice. Infertility needs proper evaluation of both partners, because it can have a lot of different causes. Some are easily fixed, some are serious, and some are time-sensitive. The right treatment depends entirely on what's actually going on, and you can't know that without getting evaluated.
