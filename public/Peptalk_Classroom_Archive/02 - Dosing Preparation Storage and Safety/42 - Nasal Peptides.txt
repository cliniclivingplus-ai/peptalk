What Are Nasal Peptides?

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/6bd3b93a?md=ef46d118dcb04931a664fcb79f0293b0

Text saved

Nasal peptides are peptides that are being delivered through an intranasal (through the nose) method of administration. 

Some peptides can work well when they are delivered through the nose, essentially creating a shortcut to the brain through the olfactory nerve, which explains why things like Semax and Selank may work well this way. 

What intranasal use can do: 

Fast absorption to the brain

Ease of use

Easy to travel with

Limitations: 

Less bioavailability 

Doesn't work for all peptides (like larger molecules or high doses)

Potential irritation in the nasal passages

Reconstituting Nasal peptides

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/6bd3b93a?md=4cf3c4b8333e4ad7aaed1182fa4a2c62

Text saved

This seems confusing, but the process is actually pretty simple. 

What You'll Need: 

Lyophilized peptide

Deionized water (preferred)

10ml air dispersal unit

Syringe to draw liquid

Alcohol Wipes

How to Determine Math: 

How much peptide is in the vial

How much liquid you add (usually 10ml)

How much liquid is dispersed per spray

Typically, it will disperse .1ml per spray. So, if you add 10ml of deionized water, a 10mg vial would disperse 100mcg, a 5mg vial would disperse 50mcg, per spray. 

How to Reconstitute: 

Add 1-3ml of deionized water into the lyophilized peptide vial. 

Transfer the reconstituted peptide into the air dispersal unit (spray bottle)

Add the remainder 7-9ml of deionized water into the air dispersal unit so a total of 10ml of liquid was added.
