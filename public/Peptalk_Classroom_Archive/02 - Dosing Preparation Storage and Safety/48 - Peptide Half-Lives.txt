What is a half-life?

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/a80f9c3e?md=86c61c8f41c54de7928e46853784cef1

Text saved

A half-life is the amount of time it takes for half of a compound to break down and clear from your system. The active concentration of the compound is being cut in half, hence half-life. 

This is important, because the half-life can play into the following: 

How often it needs to be dosed

Stability of the compound in your body (levels in the blood, etc)

How long the compound will stay in your body

If you're dosing something with a really short half-life once per week, it's basically pointless. If you're dosing a full dose of something with a long half-life, you may increase the risk of side effects. 

This is also important to understand when looking at something like Tirzepatide, and can explain why around days 4, 5, and 6, people start to feel the hunger return as you are getting to half of the active level in your system.

Half-Life Example (Retatrutide)

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/a80f9c3e?md=7f96586a8bc64ec09665a9a75822ea26

Text saved

For this example, we're going to assume a dose of 8mg per week. 

The half-life of Retatrutide is 6 days, meaning every 6 days you will half 50% less of the active compound in your system. 

The example below shows how that 8mg of Retatrutide will break down in your system over time and how long it would take for it to be completely out of your system based on the half-life period. 

Day 0 (Injection Day) - 8mg

Day 6 - 4mg

Day 12 - 2mg

Day 18 - 1mg

Day 24 - .5mg

Day 30 - .25mg 

Day 36 - .125mg

Day 42 - .0625mg

So, looking at this, after 6 days half of the amount you were at will be left in your system. After 12 days, 75% of the amount in your system will have gone away, and after 24 days, 93% of the original 8mg will be out of your system. All of this is if you do not administer another dose.



My Thoughts

https://www.skool.com/hacksmithspeptalk/classroom/a80f9c3e?md=e6839721432f49828d1ff3714b6e606b

Text saved

One thing I think is critically important when understanding half-lives, is understanding what might make the most sense when determining injection frequency for something like a GLOW blend vs. 5 on 2 off, as well as how long you might need to take a break from something like a GLP to reset your system. 

If you are using the GLOW blend, everything in that blend typically has a short half-life, and would likely be best administered 2x daily to maintain inflammation reduction benefits, etc. Doing a 5 on 2 off method for this would limit the amount of healing that you are doing because of those 2 days off, and you're not doing anything to mitigate receptor sensitivity with that 2 day break. Therefore, it makes the most sense to use something like that at a higher and more continuous frequency to maximize the benefits that you are receiving from it. 

For something like a GLP, if you're pushing almost week-long half-lives, then it is taking an incredibly long amount of time to process out of your system. Coming from something like 5mg of Tirzepatide, it will take quite a while to get down to low amounts in your system. Because of this, a 2-week break might not be long enough. Especially in a transition to something like Retatrutide. Coming off for 4-weeks, in my opinion, is even better to really let your body get closer to resetting and "feel" the effects of the Retatrutide. 

The other thing that is important to take into consideration is how something may build up in your system over time. I am going to put a picture below of Retatrutide dosed 2mg weekly to show how they will build up over the first 4-weeks. Therefore, you might not really feel the effects / benefits of Retatrutide "hit" until you get to that Week 4, 5, 6 point. That is why I always preach patience for any GLPs until that point has been reached to allow it to fully saturate in the system and get to maximum levels before trying to jump higher in dosing. 

The website for this is glp1plotter.com.
